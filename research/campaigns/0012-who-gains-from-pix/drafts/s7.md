# Task: party incumbency in close mayoral races: H1 (2020 vs 2008-2016) and H2 (by the federal bench) at the gate's window (explore, idea 0011)

## Goal

A new explore script, `explore/0011_incumbency_main.py`, on the
exploration half. It estimates idea 0011's two primary hypotheses at the
window step s6's gate chose:

- H1, the party incumbency effect in 2020→2024 against 2008-2016 pooled;
- H2, its gradient in the party's federal bench.

It also reports every cycle, the finalist-level effect and the bench
terciles the figures need. Its `results.json` carries
`estimates.finite`, the field step s8's kill rule reads. Campaign
`research/campaigns/0012-who-gains-from-pix/campaign.md`, step s7. Step s8
runs it through `explore-run`; step s11's figures read s8's record.

Idea 0011 (`research/ideas/0011-who-gains-from-pix.md`); decisions 0003,

### Facts about the data (`data/contracts/mayor_incumbency_panel.md`)

- Two rows per race, (`election`, `cd_ibge`). `win` = `margin` > 0.
- The four `*_next` columns are null where the municipality had no
  mayoral election at e+4 (2, 2 and 6 exploration-half rows in 2012,
  2016 and 2020 in PR #436's dry run). The finalist ones are also null
  where the finalist has neither CPF nor título (10 rows in 2012). A row
  with a null Y leaves that fit only; count those rows.
- `party_next` is the party at e+4 after mergers, or `F<n>` for a 2024
  federation.
- `finalist_eligible`: the finalist had not won at e-4, so is not
  term-limited at e+4.
- `bench_fed` is an int, null for 2004 and 2008, 0 where the party
  elected no federal deputy in the state.
- `amend_after_pix` is null for 2004, 2008 and 2012 (the CGU file starts
  in 2014). It is the same for both rows of a race (municipality money).

### The design (fixed)

- **Model, clustering and inference:** s5's `fit` (election-specific
  intercepts and slopes on each side, b shared within a group,
  clustered by `cd_ibge`) and `ri_draws` (2,000 race re-flips).
  - RI p = (1 + #{|draw| ≥ |observed|}) / 2001, two-sided.
  - Primary tests (H1, H2) report 97.5% intervals (b ± 2.2414 × se) and
    are judged at alpha 0.025 by their RI p.
  - Every other estimate reports a 95% interval (b ± 1.96 × se).
- **H1 (primary).** Y = `party_wins_next` (0/1). Groups pre = {2008,
  2012, 2016}, post = {2020}. Report:
  - `b_pre` and `b_post`;
  - the difference b_post − b_pre, with its clustered SE from the one
    fit, the 97.5% interval, the clustered p and the RI p.
- **H1 by election (secondary).** For each e in 2004 ... 2020, its own
  group, Y = `party_wins_next`: b, se, 95% interval, RI p, the mean of Y,
  units, races. Also the same with Y = `party_runs_next`.
- **Finalist level (secondary).** Rows with `finalist_eligible` true, Y =
  `finalist_wins_next`: each election as above, and the H1 difference
  (95% interval, RI p).
- **H2 (primary).** Y = `party_wins_next`, elections 2012, 2016 and 2020,
  B = `bench_fed` (count, untransformed):
  - add B·1[e] and win·B·1[e ∈ G] to the model, with groups pre = {2012,
    2016} and post = {2020}, b_G on `win` as before;
  - H2 = k_post − k_pre, the difference of the win × B coefficients, with
    its clustered SE, 97.5% interval, clustered p and RI p;
  - also report k_pre and k_post.
- **H2 first stage (secondary).** Y = ln(1 + `amend_after_pix` /
  `population_e`), reais per inhabitant (null where the population is
  null or 0), elections 2016 and 2020, the same model with pre = {2016}:
  k_2016, k_2020 and their difference (95% interval, RI p). 2012 is left
  out: its money is null.
- **Bench terciles (for figure 7; secondary).**
  - Cut points `cut1` and `cut2`: the 1/3 and 2/3 quantiles (lower
    interpolation) of B over the 2016 and 2020 rows within h.
  - Groups: t1 B ≤ cut1, t2 cut1 < B ≤ cut2, t3 B > cut2.
  - For e in {2016, 2020} and each group: the H1-by-election estimate on
    the rows of that group, Y = `party_wins_next` (b, se, 95% interval,
    units, races; no RI).
  - A group with fewer than 20 races in it is written null, with its
    counts, and is left out of `estimates.finite`.
- **Federations.** Report how many 2020 rows within h have a `party_next`
  starting with `F`.

- `terciles.cut1`, `terciles.cut2`, `terciles.e<2016|2020>.<t1|t2|t3>.b`,

- `estimates.finite` (bool, **k1**): true only if every number above is
  finite, except tercile groups written null by the 20-race rule.

Non-finite numbers are written as `null` and make `estimates.finite`
false.

`summary.md`:

- which gate record was read, and h;
- the H1 and H2 table (b, SE, 97.5% interval, clustered p, RI p);
- the by-election, finalist, first-stage and tercile tables;
- the caveats:
  - the question was formed after campaign 0011's exploration-half
    results and the national reelection trend;
  - `bench_fed` is predetermined, not randomised, so H2 is a gradient,
    not a second experiment;
  - 2024 federations count as one party;
  - nothing on the exploration half is a finding.

## Done when

1. A synthetic panel built in the test (five elections, 400 races each)
   recovers, within 10%:
   - a planted H1 difference;
   - a planted H2 difference in the win × B slopes;
   - with RI p above 0.05 for a null outcome under the fixed seed.
2. `gate_record` on a temporary directory:
   - with two gate records, it takes the larger run id;
   - it ignores a fork record (`# 0011_incumbency_gate_f1, run ...`) and
     a record without its `.json`;
   - it raises with no record, and with `window.found` 0.
3. An `h_pct` in the record that differs from `choose_window`'s raises.
4. Rows with a null Y leave only that fit and are counted.
5. The finalist estimates use only `finalist_eligible` rows (test).
6. Terciles:
   - the cut points follow the stated quantile rule;
   - a group under 20 races is null and does not make
     `estimates.finite` false.
7. Rows outside the exploration half are dropped before estimation
   (test).
8. `results.json` carries `estimates.finite` and parses as standard JSON.
9. `uv run ruff check .`, `uv run ruff format --check .` and `uv run pytest`
   pass.
