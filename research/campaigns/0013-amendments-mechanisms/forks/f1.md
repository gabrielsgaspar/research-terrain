# f1: Whose votes move, with predetermined race controls for sorting

## Why (from the ideation job)

Run 45034178304 gives d_party 0.0268 (SE 0.0093, RI p 0.0045), d_state 0.0322 (SE 0.0095) and d_inc 0.0337 (SE 0.0077), and the t0 placebo passes. The campaign's question 3 flags sorting: parties that already held the town hall win close races slightly more often (research/explore-runs/0011/44768996266.md, cited in the campaign file). s4's placebo checks vote levels at t0, not whether incumbent-party towns follow a different t0-to-t1 path. That path is exactly what d_party measures. The side result also shows d_state falling to 0.0194 (RI p 0.116) at the 5% check while d_party holds at 0.0274. So the whole-ticket reading (d_party about equal to d_state) is the most fragile part of the result. Adding the predetermined controls the panel already carries tests whether the declared estimates and that reading survive sorting. It reuses amendments_panel v3, so it needs no new data and no build: one explore task and one run.

## What differs from the parent

controls: add party_held_mayor, ln(population_m), round and n_mayor_cands as regressors in every party-level, deputy-level and placebo fit at h

## What a pass or a fail would mean

Pass (survived, fork): with the controls, the t0 placebo still does not fail and every number is finite. The vote result is then not explained by the sorting the campaign worries about, and the close-out can show d_party, d_state and party_minus_state with and without controls side by side. Fail (killed, fork): with controls, a t0 placebo is above 10% of its mean and significant at 5%, or some number is not finite. The party-level vote estimates would then not be robust to sorting, and the report must say that s4's reading rests on the uncontrolled model. Either way this is a further look at the same exploration half, not a finding.
