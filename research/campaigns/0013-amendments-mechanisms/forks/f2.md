# f2: When allied towns are paid, with monthly payments in ln(1 + reais per inhabitant)

## Why (from the ideation job)

Run 45036043356 (s6) passes k1 with a lead ratio of 0.0257: a pooled pre mean of 0.00602 against a post mean of 0.235 reais per inhabitant. On the level scale the estimates are lumpy and noisy. Pooled monthly SEs are 0.204 at j18 and 0.196 at j33. The post effect sits in two half-years: y_p1_h2 0.466 (SE 0.12) and y_t1_h1 0.34 (SE 0.0928), with cycle C's y_t1_h1 at 1.14 (SE 0.302). Level means per inhabitant can be driven by a few large payments to small towns. That makes the post mean large and the ratio small. The campaign's headline first stage (0.192, campaign 0011) and s2's placebo are on the ln(1 + x) scale. So the timing result has not been checked on the scale the rest of the paper uses. Only the outcome's functional form changes; the data, window, months, models and kill rules are the same. The fork uses amendments_monthly and amendments_panel v3 already in the bucket, so it needs no build: one explore task and one run.

## What differs from the parent

outcome functional form: every monthly, half-year and cumulative Y uses ln(1 + that month's reais per inhabitant) instead of reais per inhabitant

## What a pass or a fail would mean

Pass (survived, fork): on the log scale the pre-term months still carry less than half the post-term gap and every number is finite. The report can then say that the timing pattern (allied towns paid after the inauguration, not before the mayoral race) does not depend on the level scale. Fail (killed, fork): on the log scale allied towns already got noticeably more before the mayoral term (lead ratio above the inherited threshold), or a number is not finite. The s6 pass would then rest on the level scale, which large post-term payments dominate, and the report must say that the no-pretrend reading is scale-dependent. Either way it is a further look at the same exploration half, not a finding.
