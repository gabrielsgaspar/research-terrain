# Research questions

The living list of what this project is trying to learn. Edit freely; each
question that gets worked on becomes an idea file in `ideas/`.

## Core question

Does local democracy protect land that has voters and push environmental harm
onto land that does not?

City councilors in Brazil are elected by open-list proportional
representation, and their votes are geographically concentrated. If winning a
seat changes what happens around a candidate's electoral stronghold, then the
geography of representation, not just of jurisdiction, decides where the
forest is cleared.

## Sub-questions

1. **Direction by constituency.** Does representation protect forest where
   constituents value standing forest (indigenous territories) and clear it
   where they value cleared land (settlements, agrarian frontier)? The sign
   for each constituency type is fixed in the pre-analysis plan before
   outcomes are seen.
2. **Displacement.** Does clearing move to unrepresented land (undesignated
   public forest, cells far from any winner's stronghold) after a close win?
3. **Identity versus constituency.** Does an indigenous councilor behave
   differently from a non-indigenous councilor elected by the same indigenous
   voters?
4. **Mechanism.** Do winners steer public works (roads, schools, land
   regularisation) to their strongholds, and does clearing follow those works?
5. **Feedback.** Does clearing create new constituencies (new polling
   stations, new strongholds) that then demand more clearing? Descriptive
   only; no causal claim.

## What would change the plan

- Rural strongholds are rare or too coarse to measure (feasibility census).
- The pre-period placebo is not flat.
- Polling-station geocodes are too poor in rural Amazon municipalities.
